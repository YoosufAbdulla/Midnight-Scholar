Getting started
Sign in & sync
1
On the sign-in screen, tap Continue with Google to create your account. Your data syncs across every device — phone, laptop, tablet.
2
If you just want to try it first, tap Continue without account. Everything works offline — but data stays on that device only.
3
On mobile, open the ☰ menu to see your name, streak, and a Sync button. On desktop it appears in the top-right corner.
tip
The site auto-syncs every few seconds after any change. The sync icon next to your name shows a spinning arrow while saving and a green tick when done.
Navigation
1
On desktop, use the tab bar at the top to jump between pages. Every page is one click away.
2
On mobile, tap the ☰ icon (top left) to open the side menu. All pages and subjects are listed there with descriptions.
3
The Home page gives you a daily snapshot — upcoming exam countdown, today's focus topics, recent sessions, and a rotating quote.
tip
The Home countdown turns red when an exam is within 7 days and amber within 14 — so you always know what needs attention.
Spec RAG
What is RAG?
RAG stands for Red / Amber / Green. Every topic in the full specification has a coloured dot you control. Red means you don't know it, Amber means you're getting there, Green means you're confident. The goal is to get every dot green before your exam.
Unset — not reviewed yet Red — need to revise Amber — improving Green — confident
Rating topics
1
Go to Spec RAG and pick your subject (Maths, Bio, Phys, Chem) from the tabs.
2
Click a dot to cycle it: Unset → Red → Amber → Green → Unset.
3
Hold a dot for 0.6 seconds to jump straight to Green — useful when you've just revised a whole unit.
4
On mobile, swipe right on a dot to set it Green instantly, or swipe left to set it Red.
tip
Use Mark All Green / Red on a unit header to bulk-rate an entire section at once.
RAG Decay
1
Dots fade over time if you don't revisit them — Green fades to Amber after 14 days, Amber fades to Red after 21 days (adjustable in Data page).
2
A faded dot shows a lighter shade of its colour. Your saved rating is unchanged — decay is just a visual warning.
3
Click a faded dot to reset it to today's date and restore its full colour. No need to re-rate it.
tip
Topics you haven't touched in weeks show a red pulsing badge — the overdue pulse. These are your highest priority.
Spec objectives & custom topics
1
Every topic has official spec objectives underneath it. Click the arrow next to a topic name to expand and see exactly what the spec requires you to know.
2
Click + Topic (top right) to add a custom topic to any subject — useful for topics from teacher notes or past paper themes not in the official spec.
tip
Custom topics appear at the top of each subject's spec list with a custom badge. You can delete them any time.
Past Papers
Logging a paper
1
Click + Paper (top right on desktop, or in Sessions on mobile) and fill in the subject, unit, year, session, max marks, and your score.
2
Set the status — Not Started, In Progress, or Done. Your score is only used when status is Done.
3
Add a weakness note — a short phrase describing what tripped you up (e.g. "integration by parts"). This feeds the Weak Topics page.
tip
Always log every paper you attempt, even rough attempts. The Analytics page needs enough data points to show meaningful trends.
Flags & priority
1
Each paper row has a flag you can set to High, Medium, or Low priority — useful for marking papers you want to redo.
2
Your percentage score is converted to a grade automatically: A* (90%+), A (80%+), B, C, D, E, U.
3
Click the edit icon on any row to update your score, status, weakness note, or flag after the fact.
tip
The Papers page filters by subject and status — use the dropdowns to focus on just your incomplete Maths papers, for example.
Notes
Structure
Notes are organised to match the spec exactly — Paper → Unit → Topic. Every topic you rate in Spec RAG has a corresponding note page. The coloured dot next to each topic name in the tree reflects your RAG rating, so you can see at a glance where the gaps are.
Text notes
1
Select any topic from the left tree. On mobile the note panel slides in full-screen with a back button to return to the tree.
2
Type your notes in the text area under the ✍ Text tab. Click Save when done — it saves locally and syncs to the cloud.
3
Topics with a saved note show a blue indicator dot in the tree so you can quickly see what you've covered.
tip
Notes auto-save when you switch topics, but always press Save before closing the app on mobile to be safe.
Mindmaps
1
Switch to the ⬡ Mindmap tab. Click anywhere on the canvas in Node mode to place a node — type to label it.
2
Switch to Connect mode and drag from one node's edge handle to another to draw an arrow.
3
Use Draw mode for freehand annotations. Use Fit to centre everything. Pinch to zoom on mobile.
4
Zoom out until nodes turn into dots — tap a dot to zoom back in on that node instantly.
tip
Colour-code your nodes using the colour palette in the toolbar — use the subject colours (blue for Maths, green for Bio etc.) to keep things organised.
Images
1
Switch to the ⬚ Images tab and tap the + tile to upload photos — diagrams, textbook pages, handwritten notes.
2
Tap any image to open it full-screen. Swipe left/right to browse. The viewer also has a delete button inside.
tip
Take photos of diagrams you drew in class and attach them directly to the topic they belong to — much better than a separate photos app.
Community notes
1
Select any topic from the spec list on the left. Your note for that topic opens in the panel — write text, draw a mindmap, or paste images.
2
Switch to the Community tab to see notes shared by other users for that topic. Tap Save snapshot to store any into your library.
3
Saved snapshots appear under Saved from community in your My Note tab. Expand one to use just the text, just the images, or just the mindmap — independently, without overwriting anything.
tip
You own your snapshot. Tap ↺ Update shared any time to push your latest version to the community.
Sessions
Logging a session
1
Click + Session (top right on desktop, visible in Sessions on mobile). Enter how many hours you studied, what subject(s), and any notes.
2
You can log multiple subjects in one session — just type them comma-separated (e.g. "Maths, Physics").
3
Sessions feed your streak counter — study at least once per day to keep it alive. The streak dot in the topbar glows amber when your streak is active.
tip
Even a 30-minute session counts. Log 0.5 hours to keep your streak on tough days — consistency matters more than duration.
Pomodoro timer
1
Open the timer from the ⏱ button (desktop topbar) or the pomodoro button in the mobile drawer footer.
2
The default is 25 min focus / 5 min break / 15 min long break. Customise these in Settings.
3
When a focus session completes, it automatically logs a session for you — no need to do it manually.
tip
Enable browser notifications when prompted — you'll get an alert when it's time for a break even if you switch tabs.
Analytics & Weak Topics
Analytics page
1
The Score Trend chart plots your past paper scores over time per subject. Switch between All / Maths / Bio / Phys / Chem using the tabs.
2
The Subject Breakdown cards show your average score, number of papers, and grade per subject at a glance.
3
The Study Heatmap shows which days and subjects you've been spending time on — helps identify where you've been neglecting.
tip
Analytics only become meaningful with 5+ papers logged. The more data, the clearer the trend.
Weak Topics
1
The Weak Topics page automatically collects three things: Red RAG topics, overdue topics (long-decayed), and topics mentioned in your weakness notes on past papers.
2
Use this as your daily revision hit list. Everything on this page needs attention before your exam.
3
As you revise and update RAG dots or add better paper scores, topics drop off this list automatically.
tip
Check Weak Topics every morning. It changes daily as decay kicks in and new weakness notes are added — treat it as a living to-do list.
Tools & extras
Zen mode
1
Click Zen in the desktop topbar to enter full-screen focus mode — just a quote on a dark background, nothing else.
2
Use it at the start or end of a study session to clear your head. Press Esc or the close button to return.
Light / Dark theme
1
Click the moon/sun logo in the top-left to toggle between Midnight Scholar (dark) and Morning Scholar (light warm theme).
2
Your preference is saved automatically and persists across sessions and devices.
Exam Timeline
1
The Exam Timeline shows all your upcoming exams sorted by date with colour-coded subject badges and a countdown in days.
2
Click + Add on either the School or IAL section to add a custom exam — give it a subject, date, time, and duration.
3
Past exams are shown faded below. You can hide any exam you don't want cluttering the list.
tip
Add all your exams at the start of term. The Home page countdown and Today's Focus both use this data to surface the most urgent topics first.
Dashboard
1
The Dashboard gives subject-level progress — a RAG breakdown bar for each subject showing what percentage is Green, Amber, Red, or Unset.
2
The stats strip at the top shows total sessions, total hours studied, and your current streak at a glance.
3
The Focus Queue lists the most urgent topics to revise today — red topics from subjects with upcoming exams ranked by urgency.
Data & sync
Cloud sync
1
When signed in, all your data syncs automatically to the cloud. Study on your phone, review analytics on your laptop — everything stays in sync.
2
The sync icon next to your name in the drawer shows the current status — spinning = saving, green tick = saved, ⚠ = error.
3
Tap Sync in the drawer to force an immediate sync at any time.
Export & import
1
Go to the Data page (⬇ button in the minibar or drawer footer) and click Download backup.json to save a local copy of everything.
2
If you ever need to restore or move to a new browser, click Import backup.json and select your saved file.
tip
Export a backup before any major update. If you're signed in, the cloud handles this automatically — backups are only essential for offline users.
Power user tips
01
Start every revision session by checking Weak Topics. It tells you exactly what to work on without thinking about it.
02
Rate topics immediately after a lesson or past paper. Don't wait until the end of the week — fresh ratings are more accurate.
03
Use weakness notes religiously. Every time you drop marks on a past paper, log the exact topic. Weak Topics becomes your personal exam cheat sheet.
04
Build your mindmaps as you learn, not after. One node per concept, connect them as you understand the links — it forces active recall.
05
Notes auto-save when you switch topics. Use Images to paste screenshots, and Mindmap to draw connected diagrams.
06
Use the Pomodoro for past papers. Set a custom 45-min or 1hr30min timer to match real exam conditions. It logs the session automatically when done.
07
Don't let decayed topics pile up. If you see amber dots turning red, that's the site telling you it's been too long — re-visit before they stack up.
08
Photo everything. Worked examples from textbooks, diagrams from class, your own revision notes on paper — attach them to the relevant topic in Images. It becomes your personal revision bible.

Release History
v2.4 Apr 2026 new
Obsidian-style note editor — Spec Notes now uses a live-preview markdown editor. Type markdown and it renders instantly: headings, bold, italic, highlights, lists, callouts, code — all styled as you write. No separate preview mode needed.
Markdown syntax — # H1 ## H2 ### H3 for headings · **bold** *italic* ~~strike~~ · ==highlight== for yellow highlight · `code` for inline code · - and 1. for lists · > for callouts · --- for dividers.
Floating bubble toolbar — select any text and a formatting toolbar appears: Bold, Italic, Underline, Strikethrough, and 5 highlight colours (yellow, green, pink, blue, purple). Positioned above selection on desktop, pinned to bottom on mobile.
Slash commands — type / anywhere to open a command palette: H1, H2, H3, Divider, Callout, Bullet list, Numbered list, Code block, Image. Arrow keys to navigate, Enter to select.
Inline images — paste images from clipboard or drag and drop directly into the note. Images are automatically compressed (max 1200px, JPEG 72%) before being stored inline. Click an image to toggle between full-width and 60%.
Auto-save — notes save automatically 3 seconds after you stop typing. Ctrl+S (or Cmd+S) saves immediately with a green flash on the timestamp.
Mindmap tab preserved — the canvas mindmap tab is unchanged. Switch between Notes and Mindmap with the tabs at the top of the panel.
Backwards compatible — existing plain-text notes display correctly. Old HTML-formatted notes (from the previous rich-text editor) are migrated to plain text automatically on first open.
Mobile optimised — the bubble toolbar pins to the bottom of the screen above the keyboard. The slash palette appears as a bottom sheet. All tap targets are 36–44px.
v2.3 Apr 2026 improvement
Spec Notes — the Notes page is renamed Spec Notes to make it clear it's your personal, topic-linked workspace. Community sharing removed — Spec Notes are private. Share files via Resources instead.
Active users — a live green pulse in the topbar shows how many scholars are online right now. Updates in real time via Firestore presence. Hidden when you're the only one online. Shows in the drawer on mobile.
Drive connect prompt — a one-time bottom sheet appears after sign-in if Drive isn't connected. Explains what Drive is used for and lets you connect in one tap. Never shown again once connected or dismissed.
Drive token persistent — your Google Drive token is now stored in localStorage so it survives page refreshes and browser restarts. One-time auth, not once per session.
Admin panel redesigned — cleaner layout with avatar initials, coloured subject tags, stats row (files, authors, total votes), and compact action buttons. Opens as a bottom sheet on mobile. Reset button moved to footer so it's harder to hit accidentally.
Admin delete fixed — community files can now be deleted from the admin panel once the updated Firestore rules are applied (allow delete: if request.auth != null).
Community card click — clicking anywhere on a file card opens the viewer. Drive and Save buttons use stopPropagation so they still work independently.
Unshare button — when a file is shared, the button now clearly says "Unshare" with a confirmation dialog explaining the file stays in your Drive and library.
Save from community — community file cards now have a Save button. Tapping it copies the file reference into your own Notes or Worksheets tab. Shows "Saved ✓" on success.
Mobile polish — canvas toolbar scrolls horizontally on mobile, admin action buttons stack vertically on small screens, Drive prompt is a full bottom sheet on mobile, community card touch targets enlarged.
